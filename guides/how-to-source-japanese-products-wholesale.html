How to Source Japanese Daily Goods Wholesale: A Step-by-Step Guide for Importers

A practical walkthrough for retailers and importers buying Japanese drugstore and household products in bulk: building a JAN list, quotations, payment, shipping and documents.

Published: 2026-10-23

1. Decide what you want to sell, then list it by JAN

Japanese drugstore brands often sell the same product in several versions: a bottle and a refill, a regular and a large size, different scents or shade numbers. Each version has its own JAN (barcode) number. Listing products by JAN rather than by name is the single most effective way to make sure you receive exactly what you quoted.

Start with a short list of fast-moving categories in your market, for example toothpaste, shampoo, laundry detergent or skincare, and note the JAN and the quantity you want for each item. Our catalog shows the JAN on every product page.

2. Check the rules in your destination market first

Cosmetics, quasi-drugs, supplements and food are regulated differently in every country. Some products may need registration, local-language labels or an importer of record before they can be sold. Confirm these requirements with your local regulator or customs broker before you place the order; the supplier cannot do this for you.

3. Request a quotation

Send your JAN list with quantities and your destination country. A wholesale quotation normally covers unit prices, case packs (the number of units per carton), availability and an estimated shipping method and cost. With JAPANITEM there is no payment and no account needed to request a quote.

Order quantities are usually counted in full cases. If you need fewer units than a case for a test order, ask; some items can be split, others cannot.

4. Confirm, pay and ship

After you confirm the quotation you receive a proforma invoice. International B2B orders are commonly paid by bank transfer (T/T). Goods are then procured, packed and shipped by the agreed method: parcel services such as EMS for small orders, air freight for urgent medium-sized orders, or sea freight (LCL or FCL) for larger volumes.

Ask which export documents you need for customs clearance in your country, such as a commercial invoice and packing list, and whether certificates of origin or other documents are required.

5. Reorder by the same JAN

Japanese manufacturers renew packaging and formulas regularly, and a renewed item sometimes gets a new JAN. When you reorder, quote the JAN you sold before; if it has been replaced, the supplier can confirm the successor item.
