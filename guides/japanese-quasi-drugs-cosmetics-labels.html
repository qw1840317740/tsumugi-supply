Quasi-Drugs and Cosmetics: What Japanese Product Labels Mean for Importers

Many Japanese toothpastes, skincare and hand soaps are labelled 医薬部外品 (quasi-drug). What the category means in Japan and what to check before importing.

Published: 2026-10-23

Three categories under Japanese law

Japan’s Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act (薬機法) separates personal-care products into drugs, quasi-drugs (医薬部外品) and cosmetics (化粧品). Quasi-drugs contain an approved active ingredient for a specific purpose, such as preventing cavities, whitening by suppressing melanin, or disinfecting hands, and may state that effect on the package. Cosmetics are not allowed to make medicinal claims.

In our catalog this shows up in product names such as “Medicated” (薬用), for example medicated toothpaste, medicated hand soap and medicated lotions.

The destination market decides

The Japanese category does not carry over automatically. The same toothpaste may be treated as a cosmetic, an over-the-counter drug or something else in your country, depending on its active ingredients and claims. That can affect registration, labeling and who is allowed to import it.

Before ordering medicated or quasi-drug items, check the classification with your local regulator or a regulatory consultant. Keep the JAN and the Japanese ingredient list available; your consultant will usually ask for them.

Labels are in Japanese

Products made for the Japanese market carry Japanese-language labels and ingredient lists. If your market requires local-language labeling, plan for stickers or relabeling after import. We supply the products as sold in Japan and do not alter packaging.
